Shipping from China: Process and Key Considerations

A practical, step-by-step guide using kitchen cabinets as an example—from export packaging and container selection to freight terms, quality records and customs documents.

Updated August 25, 2026  ·  14-minute read

Shipping is not the final step after production. It should be planned before the purchase order is confirmed, because packing format, package dimensions, container choice, delivery term and destination access can all change the real landed cost.

Kitchen cabinets are a useful example: they are bulky, include fragile finished surfaces and hardware, and may contain hundreds of packages that must arrive in the correct installation sequence. The same workflow also applies to wardrobes, vanities and other project furniture.

The five sections below show what a buyer should confirm with a Chinese supplier and freight forwarder before shipment.

1. Goods packaging

Confirm four things before the factory starts packing: packing method, packing materials, pallet choice, and labels/shipping marks. These decisions affect product protection, container utilisation, unloading speed and installation efficiency.

2Packing materials

Build protection in layers

The material combination should match the surface, weight and handling risk. One standard carton is not enough for every cabinet component.

Surface layerProtective film or soft separator for suitable lacquer, UV, metal and high-gloss surfaces.
Impact layerEPE foam, foam blocks and rigid corner guards around exposed edges and projections.
Outer layerCorrugated or honeycomb board, export carton and stretch film.
Securing layerStraps and internal restraints for shelves, doors, drawers and hardware packs.

Pack separately when necessary: glass, mirrors, stone, sinks, handles and long decorative panels require their own protection and handling marks.

3Pallet choice

Select the pallet from the receiving conditions

Solid wood export pallets: when the destination applies ISPM 15, the pallet must carry a clear, valid IPPC treatment mark with the country code, producer or treatment-provider code, and treatment code. This is commonly called a “fumigation mark,” although the treatment may be HT (heat treatment) rather than MB fumigation. Do not accept an unmarked solid wood pallet.

4Labels and shipping marks

Make every package identifiable without opening it

The shipping mark should connect the factory order, transport documents and the buyer's receiving plan. Use one approved format throughout the shipment.

Project locationProject, building, floor, apartment and room.
Product identityKitchen type, cabinet code, colour or drawing reference.
Package identityPackage number, total packages and purchase-order reference.
Handling dataDimensions, gross weight, “This Side Up”, fragile and keep-dry symbols where applicable.

Approve before mass packing: one sample label, one sample carton and the final packing-list format. Repeat critical marks on at least two visible sides where practical.

2. Container selection

Standard dry containers are normally suitable for packaged cabinetry. The figures below are typical reference values, not booking guarantees: dimensions, door openings, tare weight and payload vary by carrier and individual container.

ContainerApprox. internal dimensions (L × W × H)Nominal volumeTypical cabinet use
20' GP5.90 × 2.35 × 2.39 mAbout 33 m³Small orders, heavy cargo or destinations where a smaller delivery batch is easier to receive
40' GP12.03 × 2.35 × 2.39 mAbout 67 m³Medium-to-large orders without high packages
40' HC / HQ12.03 × 2.35 × 2.70 mAbout 76 m³Common for volume-sensitive cabinet and furniture shipments

Nominal volume is not actual loading capacity. Carton dimensions, pallet footprint, door opening, non-stackable items, weight distribution, safety gaps, bracing and unloading sequence all reduce usable space. Ask the supplier for a package list and preliminary loading plan—not only a total CBM.

What are LCL and FCL?

ModeMeaningAdvantagesPoints to check
LCL
Less than Container Load		Your cargo shares a consolidated container with shipments from other businesses. Charges are generally based on volume or chargeable weight.Suitable for smaller orders; no need to pay for a whole container.More consolidation/deconsolidation handling, potentially longer transit, extra warehouse charges and higher packaging demands.
FCL
Full Container Load		One shipper's goods occupy the container under one FCL shipment. The container does not have to be physically full.Less cargo handling, easier sealing and loading control, often better for larger or fragile project orders.You pay at container level; compare utilisation, total route cost, free time and site unloading capacity.

There is no universal CBM at which FCL becomes cheaper. Compare complete LCL and FCL quotations for the same cargo-ready date, route and service scope.

3. Freight prices and trade terms

Ocean freight changes with route demand, vessel space, equipment availability, fuel, peak seasons, port conditions and temporary surcharges. A price comparison is only meaningful when both quotations include the same origin, destination, cargo-ready date, container type, free time and local-charge scope.

How early should you request a freight quotation?

For budgeting, obtain an indicative estimate four to eight weeks before cargo is ready. For an actionable quotation and booking, two to four weeks is a practical working window on many routes; reconfirm the rate, sailing, space and equipment when booking and again before loading. During peak periods, constrained routes or the weeks around Chinese New Year, begin four to six weeks or more in advance.

There is no fixed number of days that guarantees accuracy. Carrier quotations have their own valid-from and valid-to dates; some spot offers are valid only for hours and for a specific sailing. The quotation's written validity and booking confirmation—not the date it was requested—control whether the price can be relied on.

What do EXW, FCA, FOB and CIF mean?

TermSeller's main responsibilityBuyer should understand
EXW
Ex Works		Makes the goods available at the named premises.Buyer normally arranges loading, export procedures and transport. ICC guidance notes that EXW is primarily suited to domestic trade.
FCA
Free Carrier		Delivers the goods to the buyer's nominated carrier at the named place and clears them for export.Often the technically suitable Incoterm for containerised or multimodal shipments delivered to a terminal or carrier.
FOB
Free on Board		Loads the goods on board the nominated vessel at the named port of shipment.Risk transfers when goods are on board. FOB remains common in quotations, but the named port and exact scope must be written clearly.
CIF
Cost, Insurance and Freight		Pays cost, ocean freight and minimum insurance to the named destination port.Freight being prepaid does not mean risk stays with the seller until arrival: under CIF, risk transfers when goods are on board at origin. Destination charges, import clearance, duties and final delivery are normally not included.

Compare landed cost, not only ocean freight. Check factory pickup, origin handling, export clearance, terminal fees, ocean freight, surcharges, destination handling, customs brokerage, duties/taxes, delivery, storage, demurrage and detention.

4. What should the supplier provide?

Quality and shipment evidence

  • Approved drawings, specifications, colour and material schedule.
  • Production and pre-packing photographs for agreed checkpoints.
  • Inspection report or buyer-appointed third-party report, when required.
  • Packing photographs, package count, weights, dimensions and total CBM.
  • Container condition and loading photographs, container number and seal number.
  • Material, hardware or test certificates specifically required by the contract or destination.

Commercial and customs documents

  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list.
  • Bill of lading draft for buyer approval, then the agreed final B/L or sea waybill.
  • Sales contract or purchase order references and export declaration data.
  • Certificate of origin, insurance certificate under CIF, and wood-packaging or treatment information when applicable.
  • Any destination-specific licence, conformity document or certificate agreed before production.

Responsibility must be agreed in writing. The importer and customs broker should confirm the HS classification, import permits, taxes, product rules and exact document set for the destination. Under L/C, D/P or other documentary payment methods, document wording, dates and presentation requirements must also match the contract and banking instructions.

5. Other shipping considerations

  • Moisture: load only into a clean, dry container; select desiccants and moisture protection for the route, season and product.
  • Cargo security: control voids, weight distribution, blocking, bracing and restraint; record the seal after loading.
  • Insurance: confirm insured value, coverage, exclusions, deductible, claim deadline and evidence requirements.
  • Free time: understand demurrage, detention and storage rules before arrival.
  • Destination access: check truck restrictions, unloading labour, forklift capacity, lift sizes and temporary storage.
  • Delivery sequence: load according to the receiving and installation programme, not only factory completion order.
  • Document accuracy: approve consignee, notify party, ports, package count, weight and cargo description before the B/L cut-off.
  • Contingency: allow for port congestion, rollover, customs inspection, public holidays and project-site delays.

Final pre-shipment control: do not release the shipment until the buyer has approved the inspection result, packing list, shipping marks, container plan, quotation scope and draft transport document.

Buyer checklist before booking

  • Confirm cargo-ready date, destination and delivery address.
  • Approve assembled, KD or mixed packaging and package marks.
  • Obtain dimensions, gross weight, package count and preliminary loading plan.
  • Compare LCL and FCL on total landed cost and handling risk.
  • Choose the Incoterm and write the exact named place or port.
  • Check quotation validity, sailing, transit time, free time and excluded charges.
  • Approve inspection evidence and all draft shipping documents.
  • Confirm unloading equipment, storage space and installation sequence.
